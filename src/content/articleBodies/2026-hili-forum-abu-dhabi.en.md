On September 7–8, 2026, Jodie Wen—postdoctoral fellow and assistant research fellow at the Center for International Security and Strategy (CISS), Tsinghua University, and Director of International Communication for the China Forum—traveled to Abu Dhabi, the United Arab Emirates, at the invitation of the organizers to attend the third Hili Forum, co-hosted by the Emirates Center for Strategic Studies and Research (ECSSR) and the Anwar Gargash Diplomatic Academy (AGDA).

Under the theme "The Gulf at the Crossroads: Conflict, Consequences and Course Correction," the forum focused on new geopolitical, economic and technological developments in the Gulf region after the latest round of the U.S.-Israel-Iran conflict. It brought together political leaders, senior diplomats, leading international think-tank scholars and executives of multinational technology companies from more than 35 countries, who held more than 20 thematic sessions on key issues such as reshaping the post-war Gulf order, the security of the Strait of Hormuz, the strategic autonomy of Gulf states, and the role of small and medium-sized states in the emerging order.

During the forum, Wen was invited to take part in the parallel session "Great Power Competition and Multilateralism." The session was moderated by Robert Mason, associate professor at the Anwar Gargash Diplomatic Academy, and featured Mohammed Baharoon, director general of the Dubai Public Policy Research Center; Anthony Bubalo, Australia director of the Asia Society; and Grant Rumley, senior fellow at The Washington Institute for Near East Policy and head of its "Great Power Competition and the Middle East" project. Wen focused on the profound restructuring of international cooperation paradigms amid great power rivalry, arguing that great power competition is pushing the world away from fixed alliance systems toward more flexible, networked partnership systems. During the Cold War, countries could be clearly assigned to opposing camps; today, alliances can no longer be defined in the traditional way. Partnerships, by contrast, can form around specific issues, giving rise to a new type of cooperative partnership.

During the forum, Wen also joined several thematic discussions on the Gulf's security architecture, the transformation of its geoeconomic role, and the reconstruction of the digital order brought about by frontier and emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence. She also exchanged views with official representatives and policy figures from Europe, the United States and Middle Eastern countries—including the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Emirates Center for Strategic Studies and Research and The Washington Institute for Near East Policy—as well as with representatives of local technology institutions and companies in the Middle East.
