On January 15, 2026, a launch event for *Research on the Middle East Policy of the United States (2009–2017)*, the new book by Jodie Wen—postdoctoral fellow at the Center for International Security and Strategy (CISS), Tsinghua University, and Director of International Communication for the China Forum—was held at Tsinghua University. The event drew more than seventy scholars, experts, media professionals and young students. Those attending included Da Wei, Director of CISS; Wu Sike, member of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference and former Special Envoy of the Chinese Government on Middle East Affairs; Wang Qin, Editor-in-Chief of World Affairs Press; Liao Baizhi, Director of the Institute of Middle East Studies at the China Institutes of Contemporary International Relations; and Yu Guoqing, research fellow at the Institute of West Asian and African Studies of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences. The event was moderated by the book's author, Wen.

In his opening remarks, Professor Da Wei extended a warm welcome to the guests and offered high praise for the book from a scholar's perspective. He noted that the Middle East is a mirror for understanding U.S. foreign policy, and that U.S. policy in the Middle East has followed a clear and long-term trajectory of evolution. He said the book's systematic review and comprehensive assessment of Middle East policy during the Obama era is of considerable academic value and offers practical guidance.

Wang Qin discussed the academic impact of Dr. Wen's new book from a publisher's point of view. Drawing on the four core themes of the book, she noted that it systematically analyzes the Obama administration's strategic choices, policy instruments and actual results in the Middle East, and that, through meticulous reconstruction of historical context, it reveals the logic by which U.S. Middle East policy took shape and its long-term effects. She also reviewed many details from the editing and publication of the book.

Wu Sike shared his views from the perspective of diplomatic practice. He said the book is grand in conception: at the macro level it reviews the Middle East policies of successive U.S. administrations since the Second World War; at the meso level it systematically analyzes the Obama administration's adjustment and continuation of the Bush administration's policies; and at the micro level it offers in-depth discussion of specific issues. Drawing on his own experience on the diplomatic front line, Wu also noted that China's emergency humanitarian assistance to Middle East countries and its long-term project cooperation are compelling evidence in response to certain unfounded accusations from the international community.

Next, the book's author, Dr. Wen, delivered a keynote address introducing the book's main content. She reviewed her own path in Middle East research, described the historical evolution of U.S. Middle East strategy and the special historical juncture and strategic adjustment facing the Obama administration, and, taking U.S.–Iran relations as an example, shared her reflections on and outlook for Middle East policy in Trump's second term. Wen also expressed sincere gratitude to the teachers and editors who assisted her during the writing and publication of the book.

In the roundtable discussion that followed, the expert guests held a lively discussion on the Obama administration's Middle East policy. Liao Baizhi assessed the Obama administration's eight years of Middle East policy, arguing that it confronted two pairs of strategic choices—between democracy and allies, and between the Asia-Pacific and the Middle East—while long-term changes in the international environment and sudden conflicts in the Middle East exerted a major influence on that policy. Yu Guoqing shared his views on the Middle East strategies of major powers and on the Palestinian-Israeli issue, noting that the Obama administration's aspiration to promote democratic transformation in the Middle East ultimately went unrealized, and that multiple fissures and uncertainties remain within Middle East societies. Participating scholars explored many dimensions of the Middle East question from a range of angles and agreed that strengthening strategic assessment and academic research on the region is of great practical significance.

During the audience exchange, Li Xin and Chen Baiyu, representatives of the sixth cohort of the "Strategic Youth" program, shared their reading reflections in light of their respective academic backgrounds. Speaking from the angle of "telling a good story," Li Xin shared her understanding of the Middle East predicaments and strategic choices the Obama administration faced over its eight years in office, and of the dynamic interaction among doctrine, ideas, alliances and military power; she said the book had been highly instructive for the narrative logic and structural coherence of her own academic writing. Chen Baiyu, drawing on his background in computer science, shared his long-standing attention to developments in the Middle East and said that Dr. Wen's new book is rigorous in structure, systematic in content, and forward-looking and instructive in its conclusions, offering important insights for readers seeking to understand the Obama administration and U.S. foreign policy more broadly. The scholars present also responded to audience questions on Middle East oil, terrorism governance and the prospects for artificial intelligence development. The event concluded successfully in a warm and in-depth atmosphere of academic exchange.

Through the in-depth discussion across disciplines and perspectives, the launch event not only deepened understanding of U.S. Middle East policy during a particular period, but also fostered a valuable dialogue among academic research, policy practice and public understanding.
