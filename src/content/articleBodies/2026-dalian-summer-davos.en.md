From June 23 to 25, 2026, Jodie Wen, postdoctoral fellow and assistant research fellow at the Center for International Security and Strategy (CISS), Tsinghua University, and Director of International Communication for the China Forum, was invited to attend the 2026 Annual Meeting of the New Champions of the World Economic Forum (the Summer Davos Forum), held in Dalian.

This year's Summer Davos took “Scaling Innovation” as its theme, focusing on global issues such as geopolitics and economics, global supply chain resilience, geopolitical risk management, and digital economy governance, and bringing together political leaders, executives of multinational companies, think-tank experts and academic representatives from various countries to discuss new pathways for global economic growth. During the forum, Premier Li Qiang of the State Council delivered a keynote address at the opening ceremony, setting out China's position and measures on promoting the deep integration of technological innovation with industry, deepening high-standard opening up, and advancing practical international cooperation, which prompted in-depth discussion among the participants.

During the forum, Wen was invited to take part in three closed-door thematic discussions, namely “China–MENA Cooperation,” “AI and Future Growth,” and the “Women Leaders Summit.” In the closed-door exchange on China–MENA cooperation, Wen focused on sharing her views on the current situation in the Middle East and on the prospects for cooperation between China's development opportunities in the opening year of its 15th Five-Year Plan and the economic diversification of Middle Eastern countries; in the closed-door discussion on artificial intelligence, she shared the priorities of Gulf countries such as Saudi Arabia and the UAE in artificial intelligence and the digital economy, as well as the prospects for cooperation between China and the Middle East.

During the forum, Wen also attended a number of public sessions, including “China–US Relations,” “The Future of the Middle East,” “The Situation in Iran,” and “The Renminbi and the World,” where she exchanged views with scholars and policy figures from universities and think tanks such as Harvard University, Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, and Chatham House in the United Kingdom, as well as with the heads of international business at Chinese multinational companies with deep roots in the Middle East market, such as Lenovo and Chery. She surveyed the current state of digital project implementation by Chinese companies in the Middle East, especially in the Gulf region, and collected first-hand practical cases, building up primary research material for subsequent studies on strategic interaction among major powers in the Middle East, the region's strategic transformation, and related topics.
