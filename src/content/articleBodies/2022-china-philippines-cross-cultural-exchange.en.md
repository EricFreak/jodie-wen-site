## Editor's Note

On December 4, the China–Philippines Gen-Z Cross-Cultural Online Dialogue was successfully held. The event was co-hosted by the Beijing People's Association for Friendship with Foreign Countries and the Association for Philippines-China Understanding, with the support of China Energy Engineering Corporation and Huanqiu.com. At the dialogue, scholars and Gen-Z representatives from China and the Philippines each shared their views and suggestions on strengthening China–Philippines youth exchange from their own perspectives. This article is a selection of the guests' remarks.

Thank you to the organizers for the invitation. I am delighted to have the opportunity to meet and exchange views with friends from China and the Philippines, even if only by video. The COVID-19 pandemic has created many obstacles to international exchange and interaction, but the internet has brought us closer, enabling us to keep communicating.

Before I formally begin today's speech, I would like to share two small stories about my own interactions with Filipino friends. The first dates back to 2016, when I was still working in the media and co-founded the China–ASEAN Youth Summit with Professor Zhai Kun of Peking University. At that time I met many Filipino students studying in China; they were warm and cheerful in character and took an active part in the summit, which left a deep impression on me. The second small story is from 2018. Peking University held a celebration for its international alumni on the occasion of its 120th anniversary, and I had the honor of serving as host. One of the guests was Mr. Jaime, who had shortly before been appointed by President Marcos as the new Philippine Ambassador to China. Jaime had worked and lived in Beijing for decades; he is not only a PKU alumnus but also the founding president of the Peking University International Students Alumni Association. Over the years, Jaime has made many contributions to advancing China–Philippines exchange. What I want to say is that it is precisely Ambassador Jaime and the many students from both China and the Philippines who have contributed their individual efforts to China–Philippines cross-cultural exchange, laying a very good foundation for friendly relations between the two countries.

If we trace the history of exchanges between China and the Philippines, we find that cultural exchange between the two countries goes back a long way. The earliest documented exchanges began in the Song Dynasty, when the two countries carried out people-to-people exchanges through maritime trade. During the Ming Dynasty, in the period of the King of Sulu in the Philippines, bilateral relations reached their peak. Many overseas Chinese from Fujian and Guangdong became the main practitioners of China–Philippines cultural exchange. On June 9, 1975, China and the Philippines formally established diplomatic relations. In November 2018, President Xi Jinping paid a state visit to the Philippines, and the leaders of the two countries agreed to establish a comprehensive strategic cooperative relationship. In May this year, after President Marcos took office as President of the Philippines, the leaders of the two countries reached a series of important understandings by telephone on the development of China–Philippines relations in the new era, and the two sides identified four priority areas of cooperation, including culture and people-to-people exchange. Half a month ago, President Xi Jinping and President Marcos met in Thailand, and the two sides reaffirmed that they will expand cooperation in various fields and build a more robust and dynamic bilateral relationship. President Xi Jinping also made clear that the two sides should take more concrete measures to strengthen people-to-people and cultural exchange and consolidate the public foundation of China–Philippines friendship. It is fair to say that the long-term stable and friendly relations between China and the Philippines in the political and diplomatic fields are the very foundation of cross-cultural exchange between the two sides.

Economic and trade cooperation is the "propeller" and the "ballast" of the development of relations between the two countries, and it promotes cross-cultural exchange between them. In 2021, bilateral trade between China and the Philippines had reached US$82.05 billion. China is the Philippines' largest trading partner, largest source of imports and second-largest export market. On October 27, 2022, a grand groundbreaking ceremony was held for the Davao–Samal Island Bridge project, a flagship project of China–Philippines intergovernmental cooperation. The network coverage of the Philippines' third telecommunications operator, in which China Telecom holds a stake, has reached nearly 70 percent, with nearly 9 million active users. In addition, a number of Chinese high-end manufacturing enterprises have come to the Philippines to invest and set up factories, and the high-tech company Alibaba Cloud has already completed the construction of a data center in the Philippines. Cooperation between China and the Philippines in the political, economic and other fields has provided rare opportunities for cross-cultural exchange between the two countries.

But we should also see the challenges facing China–Philippines cross-cultural exchange. The current world order is undergoing drastic change, and the overlapping effects of the Ukraine crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic have reached the Asia-Pacific region as well. The United States, for its part, still clings to geopolitical and zero-sum thinking, regards China as a competitor, and hypes the "China threat" narrative at the global and regional levels. The disputes between China and the Philippines in the South China Sea are sensitive and complex, and there are still many differences between the two sides. How to avoid the influence of major powers from outside the region and properly handle disputes is the key to steadily advancing China–Philippines cross-cultural exchange in the post-pandemic era.

## Finally, I would like to offer a few thoughts on China–Philippines cross-cultural exchange in light of today's forum theme:

First, uphold the principles of mutual respect, openness and inclusiveness, and mutual learning. Cross-cultural exchange between China and the Philippines has not been all smooth sailing: the two countries differ in language, customs and habits, religious beliefs and ideology, and people-to-people and cultural exchange has always been affected by the two countries' politics and diplomacy. The two sides should strengthen communication, advance understanding, and strive to find the common ground in exchanges and interactions between China and the Philippines.

Second, expand the channels and areas of exchange. For example, cross-cultural exchange between the two countries can help strengthen exchanges at the provincial and municipal levels. China and the Philippines have 34 pairs of sister provinces and cities, such as Guangzhou and Manila, and Xiamen and Cebu, and we should actively promote the establishment of cooperation mechanisms for cross-cultural exchange between the two sides and build more bridges for exchange and communication.

Third, engage the youth and pool the strength of "Gen Z." As a new force influencing social development, Gen Z is the most dynamic group today and also the main force of cross-cultural exchange. Only by fully pooling the strength of Gen Z can China–Philippines cross-cultural exchange truly be full of vitality.

Fourth, give full play to the role of the media. The media, especially the mass media, are a tool for advancing the political process and an important vehicle for cross-cultural exchange between countries. As far as I understand, Filipinos rank first in the world in social media use. Since the pandemic, demand for the internet in the Philippines has increased, and social media platforms have become increasingly active. As of January 2022, the total population of the Philippines was 111.8 million, of whom 92.05 million were social media users, equivalent to 82.4 percent of the total population. Gen Z is known as the native generation of the mobile internet, and we should encourage and advocate that Gen Z in China and the Philippines build more connections through social media.

Last November, the G20 meeting and the APEC meeting were held in succession in Indonesia and Thailand, and the world believes that Asia is enjoying a "highlight moment." China and the Philippines are both developing countries in Asia, and I hope the two sides can give full play to the strength of Gen Z and join hands to create a "golden moment" of cross-cultural exchange!
