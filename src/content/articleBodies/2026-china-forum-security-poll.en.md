On January 15, 2026, the China Forum of the Center for International Security and Strategy (CISS) at Tsinghua University successfully held the ninth session of the "China Forum Expert-Media Dialogue." This session focused on the release of CISS's latest public opinion survey report, "2025 Chinese Public Opinion on International Security," and invited experts and scholars from the Carter Center and the Chicago Council on Global Affairs in the United States, as well as representatives of foreign media in China, to join in-depth exchanges on the latest perceptions of the Chinese public regarding international security issues.

Da Wei, director of CISS and professor in the Department of International Relations at Tsinghua University, moderated the opening session. Dong Ting, associate research fellow at CISS, presented the survey's core findings to the attending experts and media reporters and offered her reading of some of the shifts in perception. Jodie Wen, postdoctoral fellow at CISS and director of international communication for the China Forum, moderated the expert discussion and the media interaction session. Liu Yawei, senior adviser for China at the Carter Center; Nick Zeller, executive editor of US-China Perception Monitor; and Dina Smeltz, director and chair of public opinion and foreign policy at the Chicago Council on Global Affairs, each shared their institutions' survey findings in the relevant fields and held in-depth discussions with Da Wei and Dong Ting on the survey's main conclusions, its methodology and other questions.

Media representatives attending the event came from the Financial Times, Der Spiegel, Bloomberg, Brazil's O Globo, Rossiya Segodnya, The Asahi Shimbun, NHK, Lianhe Zaobao and the South China Morning Post.

Chinese Public Opinion on International Security is an annual survey report that CISS has released since 2023, intended to present, continuously and objectively, the latest perceptions of the Chinese public on international security issues to the world.
