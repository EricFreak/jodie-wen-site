On May 12, 2026, the China Forum of the Center for International Security and Strategy (CISS) at Tsinghua University successfully held the 11th "China Forum Expert-Media Dialogue." Under the theme "Previewing the Xi–Trump Summit: Where Do China–US Relations Go from Here?", the participating experts held in-depth discussions and exchanges on the core issues likely to be on the agenda at the meeting between the two heads of state, and on the far-reaching implications that the meeting will have for the future development of China–US relations.

The participating experts held that the core significance of this Xi–Trump summit lies in setting a clear direction, stabilizing the relationship and managing risks, and in providing a crucial buffer and communication platform for the ties between the two countries. As the main items on the China–US agenda shift from traditional trade and economic issues toward strategic domains such as crisis management and global AI governance, defining the boundaries of competition clearly will be key to the dialogue. In addition, the experts held in-depth discussions on, and answered journalists' questions about, issues including the potential impact of the Middle East situation on China–US relations, the future trajectory of China–US relations, and China's role in regional and global security frameworks.

Da Wei, Director of CISS; Zhou Bo, CISS research fellow and special expert of the China Forum; Xie Tao, Dean of the School of International Relations at Beijing Foreign Studies University; Shao Yuqun, Director of the Institute of Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao Studies at the Shanghai Institutes for International Studies; and Denis Simon, senior research fellow of the East Asia Program at the Quincy Institute, delivered keynote remarks. Jodie Wen, CISS postdoctoral fellow and assistant research fellow and Director of International Communication for the China Forum, moderated the media Q&A session.

Media representatives attending the event came from Reuters, the Associated Press, Bloomberg News, Brazil's O Globo, the UK's The Guardian, Russia's TASS, Russia's Sputnik, Morocco's Maghreb Arabe Presse, Japan's Kyodo News, The Economist, The Asahi Shimbun, Radio Netherlands Worldwide, Germany's Der Spiegel, Radio Netherlands Worldwide, The Yomiuri Shimbun, The New York Times, Lianhe Zaobao, the South China Morning Post, TVB, China.org.cn and others.
