## Abstract

The 2024 US election highlighted the profound reconstruction of the political communication ecosystem in the digital age, with the two parties' candidates adopting differentiated media strategies. The Trump team displayed “decentralized” features in its media communication: it relied on Truth Social to build a closed information ecosystem and consolidate die-hard supporters, used TikTok to break through generational barriers, and achieved ideological penetration through a podcast alliance; the Harris team, by contrast, pursued a “dual-track” strategy, upholding the authoritative narrative of legacy media while using short-video platforms to give policy issues an entertaining encoding, and drawing on identity politics to attract support from ethnic minorities and young voters. The communication landscape under technological empowerment shows structural change: legacy media's agenda-setting power is dissolved by algorithmic politics, social media has become the main battlefield of cognitive warfare, and the deep companionship of podcasts has reconstructed the way American political discourse is expressed. Facing the deep fragmentation of the American public opinion sphere and the spread of disinformation, communication strategies and paths are showing new changes. China needs to build a new type of international communication governance system and, by strengthening cross-actor collaborative communication, promoting the global layout of its own platforms, and innovating visual narrative strategies, break through the “strong West, weak China” discourse predicament.

## Key Points

1. In the 2024 US election, the Trump team adopted a “decentralized” media strategy, building through Truth Social, TikTok, and podcasts a communication network that runs closed and breakout channels in parallel.
2. The Harris team implemented a “dual-track” strategy, relying on legacy media to maintain an authoritative narrative while using short-video platforms to spread content that is entertaining and framed through identity politics.
3. Legacy media's agenda-setting power has been weakened; social media and podcasts have become the main battlefield of political communication, and algorithms have reinforced information cocoons and cognitive division.
4. The MAGA camp has built a closed-loop communication system through proprietary platforms, conservative media, and a podcast alliance, strengthening ideological penetration and voter mobilization.
5. China should build a new type of international communication system, strengthen cross-actor coordination, promote its own platforms abroad, and innovate visual narratives in order to break the Western monopoly on discourse power.
6. It is necessary to proactively set the international agenda and avoid responding passively, and to explore diversified, cross-cultural communication methods so as to enhance the international acceptance of Chinese discourse.

## Introduction

Against the backdrop of domestic political polarization and fragmentation in the United States and the wave of technological innovation triggered by artificial intelligence, the 2024 US election became an important window into the changes in the political communication ecosystem of the digital age. In this election, the contest between legacy media and emerging digital platforms grew increasingly intense, and artificial intelligence technology became deeply integrated with short-video communication, gradually reshaping the pathways for transmitting political information and the ways voters are mobilized. As social media algorithmic recommendation mechanisms strengthen day by day, the information cocoon effect becomes more pronounced, and voters show clear generational divergence in media exposure. Younger generations are more inclined to obtain information through short-video platforms and podcasts, while the audience base of legacy media continues to shrink. This structural change in audiences forces campaign teams to re-examine and adjust their communication strategies in order to adapt to the fragmenting and instantaneous trends of the information age.

In this election, the two parties' candidates showed completely different path choices in media strategy. The Trump team continued its consistent “anti-establishment” line, placing greater emphasis on conservative values and committing itself to building a communication system independent of the mainstream media, forming a tight communication chain through social media and conservative podcasts to mobilize its core supporters directly. The Harris camp, by contrast, placed greater emphasis on diversity and inclusiveness and adopted a “dual-track parallel” strategy: on the one hand, it relied on legacy media to interpret policy issues authoritatively; on the other, it actively moved into short-video platforms, trying to attract more young voters with light, interactive content.

The article will compare and analyze the media policies of the Trump and Harris campaign teams and, combined with specific cases, explore the evolution of the roles played by legacy and emerging media in this campaign. At the same time, by sorting out the representative communication channels of the American MAGA community and their operating characteristics, it will show the fragmentation of the American public opinion sphere, and finally, on this basis, propose China's response strategies.

## I. Old Versus New Media Strategies: An Analysis of the Trump and Harris Campaign Teams' Strategies

### (1) Trump's “Bypass the Mainstream” Media Tactics

Trump's media strategy takes “decentralization” as its core logic, aiming to break the agenda-setting power of traditional mainstream media and build an autonomous, controllable communication matrix. Some scholars have reviewed the evolution of legacy media's role in the last three US elections, arguing that starting with the 2016 election, legacy and emerging media came into their first confrontation, moved toward convergence in the 2020 election, but in the 2024 election legacy media showed a clear trend of weakening. This strategy can be examined from three dimensions.

In terms of platform choice, Trump displays a strategic logic of using self-built platforms to consolidate die-hard supporters and mass platforms to compete for the middle ground, the core of which is to build a multi-layered, multi-dimensional communication network. First, with Musk reinstating his X (Twitter) account, Trump reactivated the communication efficacy of this public opinion arena, using its openness and immediacy to bypass legacy media's “information filter” directly and to ensure that populist narratives could penetrate the barriers of mainstream public opinion. Second, he joined TikTok, reshaping a “man of the people” image through slice-of-life short videos; within less than four hours on the account's first day, his followers exceeded 400,000, winning the support of young voters and achieving a breakout effect in generational communication. Finally, through his self-built social media platform Truth Social, he strengthened the fortress role of his own position, ensuring that his campaign issues could be communicated smoothly and reach voters, forming a closed communication ecosystem and consolidating the loyalty of core supporters. In addition, Trump attached great importance to the role of podcast platforms. For example, he appeared on the podcast of Joe Rogan, the most-listened-to host in the United States, using a three-hour conversation to build listeners' positive perception of Trump and to win more support from young male voters in the election.

In terms of content and key audiences, Trump achieved precise penetration of key voter groups in the 2024 election through a “tiered media strategy”. Data show that in the composition of Trump's supporters, white voters remain the main body, but the Black group nearly doubled compared with 2020. Trump's posts usually consist mainly of short speeches, quotes from public events, and slogans, accompanied by videos of him appearing at events and campaign rallies and interacting with voters. Trump's posts also extensively cited posts by influencers with large followings, such as Logan and Jake Paul. For white base voters, Truth Social, through a closed information cocoon, continuously delivered Trump's more extreme political rhetoric—for example, denigration of the previous president and attacks on Harris—consolidating the support of fervent voters. This multi-track parallel communication strategy breaks through voter boundaries via mass platforms while reinforcing ideological identification through vertical media, ultimately building a cross-group “populist consensus”.

### (2) Harris's “Dual-Track” Media Strategy

The Harris team's media strategy shows typical “establishment” features, its core being the collaborative operation of legacy media and emerging platforms to build a composite communication system of “authoritative endorsement—youth outreach”. This strategy both continues the Democratic Party's traditional path of building policy legitimacy through mainstream media and actively adapts to a model of youth political participation dominated by short-video platforms. In terms of platform choice, the strategy focused on two dimensions: on the one hand, it held fast to the establishment media matrix, maintaining the seriousness and credibility of policy interpretation through authoritative news programs such as “60 Minutes” and reaching middle-aged and older core voters through traditional television networks such as ABC and CBS; on the other hand, it actively penetrated podcasts and social platforms, appearing on leading audio programs such as “Call Her Daddy” while posting video content suited to young fans' tastes on TikTok, achieving all-scenario coverage of young voters. This “centralized—decentralized” parallel media strategy combines mainstream media's agenda-setting capacity with emerging platforms' effect of penetrating social circles. For example, Harris remixed audio from podcast interviews and spliced together the more brilliant images from her career, posting them on TikTok to help shape her personal image.

At the level of content and key audiences, Harris adopted an “issue binding—identity awakening” communication strategy, implementing precise content placement by segmenting audiences' information consumption characteristics. For ethnic minority groups, she used Black radio programs as her base, discussing systemic racism in the form of informal conversation and integrating policy positions into narratives of cultural identity. For example, through an interview with the Black host Charlamagne Tha God, she focused on topics of concern to Black voters in order to raise her support. For young female voters, on “Call Her Daddy” she took “sexual autonomy—abortion rights” as her entry point, pointing to Trump's poor record on abortion and women's rights. It is worth noting that her team deliberately downplayed the traditional style of political discourse and did not directly promote its own political positions; instead, it packaged and embedded its ideas in the content. This communication paradigm of “domesticating policy issues” successfully constructed a media image of “spokesperson for progressive values” among Gen Z, offering a new path for breaking through the generational turnout dilemma.

## II. The Changing Landscape of Communication Channels: The Decline of Legacy Media and New Media as the Main Battleground

### (1) The Decline of Legacy Media: The Structural Dissolution of Agenda-Setting Power

In this election, traditional mainstream media displayed a “fading authority” predicament, with their core function evolving from agenda leaders into passive responders. By systematically avoiding contact with legacy media, Trump directly deconstructed the legitimacy foundation of establishment media. His refusal to take part in mainstream televised debates and his boycott of interviews with liberal media such as The New York Times were essentially a negation of legacy media's “gatekeeper” role. This strategy reflects a deeper shift in communication logic: when social media gives candidates the ability to “build their own communication matrix”, the authoritative narrative constructed by legacy media through issue selection and fact-checking can no longer monopolize public cognition. Harris's media strategy, in turn, highlighted the failure of the traditional path: despite huge television advertising spending in five swing states, her policy messages failed to penetrate voters' information barriers. This exposes the fatal flaw of traditional political communication: a linear communication model struggles to cope with a fragmented information ecosystem, and one-way policy advocacy cannot activate voters' emotional identification. More critically, legacy media's efforts in fact-checking and in-depth reporting were instead constructed by populist discourse as evidence of “elites manipulating public opinion”, aggravating voters' crisis of trust in establishment media.

### (2) New Media as the Main Battleground: The Convergence of Algorithmic Politics and Cognitive Warfare

Social media platforms evolved in this election into an “algorithm-empowered political theater”, with candidates reconstructing voters' cognitive landscapes through multimodal content strategies. The Trump team used TikTok to culturally encode populism through a series of visual narratives; the Harris camp also attracted more young voters through entertaining content. The essence of this content strategy is to use platforms' algorithmic “preference reinforcement” mechanism and achieve ideological penetration through micro-level information filtering. In addition, unlike China's media ecosystem, podcasts are also an important arena of candidates' political contestation in the election. As a deep communication medium, podcasts, with their features of “co-present exchange” and “companion reception”, have become a key tool for candidates to break out of information cocoons. Their intimate register dissolves the alienation of traditional political discourse; hosts guide candidates to explain policy in conversational form, giving listeners the immediacy of a “living-room conversation”, which is conducive to shaping candidates' personal image and promoting their political positions. However, the technical features of social media have spawned “cognitive guerrilla warfare”: anonymous accounts, deepfake videos, and algorithmic recommendation form a synergy, filling the public opinion sphere with a large number of false narratives that can easily influence swing voters' choice of position. When disinformation spreads emotionally through social relationship chains, even rational voters who can recognize its absurdity may shift their cognition in conformity under group pressure. The “echo chamber effect” of platform algorithms further solidifies cognitive division.

## III. The Media Communication Paths and Platform Characteristics of MAGA Ideas in the United States

The MAGA (“Make America Great Again”) camp, as a right-wing populist force in the American political spectrum, relies heavily in its communication ideas on a decentralized, vertical media network. By building a composite communication matrix of “proprietary platforms + conservative media + podcast alliance”, this group has achieved precise reach to core supporters and ideological reinforcement.

### (1) Closed-Loop Communication on Proprietary Platforms

Truth Social, a social platform under Trump Media & Technology Group (TMTG), has presented itself as a “guardian of free speech” since its launch in 2022; it is the proprietary stronghold of the MAGA camp and conservatives. Its core positioning is to build a conservative digital enclave “free of liberal bias”. The platform reinforces a closed communication ecosystem through algorithmic design: its default feed, “Truth Feed”, prioritizes posts by Trump, his supporters, and conservatives, while restricting external links to block the infiltration of mainstream media information. Roxana Muenster, a Cornell University scholar studying far-right digital communication, pointed out that Truth Social's operating logic has a clear ideological adversarial character: “Its marketing target is opposition to mainstream media apps, which Trump and his supporters claim discriminate against their views and restrict free speech.”

### (2) Conservative Strongholds in Legacy Media

Fox News is the traditional stronghold the Trump team built in legacy media; through an around-the-clock programming system it has constructed a conservative communication matrix, and its political orientation has shifted from implicit expression of positions to institutionalized agenda setting. The morning current-affairs program “Fox & Friends” implants conservative values in the form of “kitchen-table conversation”, while the afternoon debate program “The Five”, through role-based debate design, hardens the cognitive frame of “the liberal threat”. The hosts of the three flagship prime-time programs—Jesse Watters, Sean Hannity, and Laura Ingraham—built a multidimensional system centered on Trump that critiques Democratic policies, forming a communication chain from morning opinion groundwork to nighttime reinforcement of viewpoints. In terms of personnel appointments, Lara Trump, Trump's daughter-in-law, also joined Fox News, promoting the MAGA camp's positions and views through the interview program “My View with Lara Trump” and conversations with thought leaders.

### (3) Deep Penetration and Contextualized Communication through the Podcast Alliance

With their “companion reception” and “intimate register”, podcasts have become a key tool for the MAGA camp to break out of information cocoons. Leading programs such as The Dan Bongino Show, through the supply of exclusive audio clips, give full expression to the MAGA camp's voice. For example, the podcast episode of November 20, 2024, presented examples of MAGA's cultural takeover and the collapse of legacy media that followed. In addition, “The Joe Rogan Experience” is also an important platform for the MAGA camp to voice its views. Joe Rogan cited the Arizona textbook review case and Texas's “anti-woke” legislative practice, packaging them as “the return of traditional American spirit”, while attacking institutions such as The New York Times for falling into a “death spiral of self-censorship” due to plummeting subscriptions.

## IV. Response Strategies: Building a New International Communication Governance System

Facing the new changes the two parties showed in communication strategies and communication channels in the 2024 US election, China needs to build a new type of international communication governance system and, by strengthening cross-actor collaborative communication, promoting the global layout of its own platforms, and innovating visual narrative strategies, break through the “strong West, weak China” discourse predicament.

### (1) Strengthening International Communication Capacity to Break the “Strong West, Weak China” Discourse Predicament

The current international public opinion situation is severe and complex, and there is a considerable gap between China's perception of itself and the international community's perception of China. We should maintain strategic resolve while also possessing tactical flexibility, and build an efficient international communication system. We can explore a collaborative communication mechanism among media, think tanks, enterprises, and civil society, avoiding the situation of “each speaking from its own corner” in shaping public opinion, achieving comprehensive, three-dimensional, full coverage, and creating a communication effect of “one voice” and “all horses silent”.

### (2) Taking Proprietary Media Platforms Global to Build “New Infrastructure” for Global Communication

Because of the US TikTok ban bill, China's Xiaohongshu platform attracted a large number of “TikTok refugees”, with downloads and usage hitting a record high, becoming a vivid practical platform for people-to-people exchange and dialogue between the two countries. This inspires us to optimize the “localized” operating strategies of platforms such as TikTok and Xiaohongshu, raise their share of overseas markets, and ensure that there is a sufficient Chinese voice in the international public opinion sphere. Specifically, regional elements can be added to algorithmic recommendation: in the Southeast Asian market, focus on “e-commerce poverty alleviation” cases; in the Latin American market, focus on “digital health cooperation”; and deliver information according to the different characteristics of each region, using micro-narratives to dissolve macro-level prejudice.

### (3) Proactively Setting the Agenda and Avoiding Passive “Defense”

The initiative in agenda setting determines the dominant advantage in international communication. In external communication, we should avoid merely giving negative responses to Western rhetoric and should proactively set the agenda through narrative innovation. As the audience of communication, the subject of public opinion enjoys a first-mover advantage; the party that sets the agenda first often has greater initiative in determining the direction of events. Faced with narrative traps designed by the United States, we need to stay problem-oriented, use political terminology widely accepted in the West to increase the acceptability of our discourse, identify the consensus points of the international community, and create original discourse and narrative leadership. We should avoid falling into the other side's agenda trap and engaging in endless “defense”. Regarding China-related disinformation in US partisan competition, we should not only respond point by point and debunk rumors but also proactively set the agenda, so as not to become a victim of US partisan competition.

### (4) Exploring Innovative Forms of Discourse and Diversifying Communication Tools

External communication actors can use diversified communication methods such as video, podcasts, text, and panel discussions to explore forms of discourse expression and attract more audiences. In line with the different goals of external communication, flexible communication methods can be designed, using connectable international communication platforms to innovate narrative approaches and communication forms. We can try to present cultural symbols with Chinese characteristics visually through social media platforms such as TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and X. For example, using animation and other forms, policies such as “common prosperity” and “rural revitalization” can be transformed into visual narratives, focusing on visual presentation and starting from the excavation of stories to find angles of cross-cultural empathy, thereby reducing resistance to the external communication of Chinese elements.

## Conclusion

The media games in the 2024 US election profoundly revealed the reshaping effect of digital technology on modern democratic politics. Under the dual action of algorithmic recommendation and information cocoons, political communication has evolved into a cross-platform, multimodal contest over cognition; the transfer of power between legacy media and social platforms reflects the deep crisis of a generational cognitive gap and value fissure. The practice of the 2024 US election shows that political mobilization is shifting from one-way policy advocacy toward immersive, two-way emotional connection, and that voters' decisions are increasingly affected by a fragmented information ecosystem. This transformation of the communication ecosystem not only aggravates the disintegration of American social consensus but also has a profound impact on the international public opinion landscape. China needs to respond to challenges with strategic foresight: it should be alert to the negative effects of US political polarization on global governance, and more importantly, seize the opportunities of technological change—by cultivating its own digital platforms, building a discourse system with Chinese characteristics, and proactively setting the agenda—to gain the initiative in the international public opinion arena.
